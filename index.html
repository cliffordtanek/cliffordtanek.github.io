04 About

I started in architecture — a diploma at Ngee Ann, then National Service, then a full-time job as an architectural assistant. It took working in the field to realise it wasn't the right fit, so I switched into computer science and went back to being a first-year.

That detour turned out to be useful. A lot of what I work on now is spatial: parcelling land into buildable plots, moving a crowd of agents through terrain, laying out a level so a player learns a mechanic without being told. The tools changed; the way of thinking didn't.

These days I write robotics and embedded software at DSO. I like the parts of engineering where you can't hand-wave — where a bus either syncs or it doesn't, a frame either lands in 16ms or it doesn't. Outside work I'm usually on a basketball or badminton court, in the gym, or somewhere I haven't been before.
